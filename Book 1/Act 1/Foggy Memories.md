## Overview

The players have wandered into the Dreamlands, their memories stolen from them. Worse still, they are being hunted, one by one, by the Tatterman. When he catches them, and not if, he will kill them in the most horrific ways imaginable.

Until that happens, the dreamscape they find themselves in is a place where anything, everything, and even things that should not be possible can happen.

## Read-Aloud

> All around you is a wall of sickly yellow fog, tumbling through the alley's canyon of crumbling grey brick walls like some jaundiced flash flood. Ahead, the unfamiliar alley splits, curving away to the left and right. Behind you, from the silent swell of mist, comes the sound of footsteps. Slow, yet somehow keeping pace with the careening, hungry wave.
> 
> You don't remember. Not what came before, not how you got here. You don't even remember the others, who, like you, now find themselves being hunted...

## GM Brief

Don't kill the players too quickly. Give them a moment to decide what to do and encourage them to use their tokens to explore the space.

If you feel up to it, have Robert start in another area of the maze and pursue the players. If he catches someone, narrate what happens. Otherwise, wait until the most dramatically appropriate moment to spring him on them.

Once he kills the first player, however, keep up the pressure. Try to get the remaining players running and separated.

When the first player is killed, reveal the **ME** sign, then the **UP**, followed by the **SAVE**, and finally the **WAKE!** The word ME, in particular, echoes forever once it has been triggered.

When players encounter the various things scattered throughout the maze, they may be alone or accompanied by someone else. As they come across these scenes, first ask one of the players to describe what they see. There is a very real possibility that they will see nothing at all.

This is intentional. The map is designed so that only certain people can see certain things, splitting the group up and leaving them confused about what is happening around them.

If the players ask for items, remember that they receive 1d4 items.

> 
> It is almost tranquil. You feel a gentle summer breeze, and for a moment, everything seems peaceful.

## Purpose

The whole point of this section is to establish the tone and atmosphere of the entire campaign. It is about misdirection, weirdness, and the unpredictable nature of dreams and the King in Yellow.

The players should never feel entirely certain of what they are seeing, where they are, or whether the things happening around them are even possible.

## Goal for Players

There isn't one. They will all die here, and there is nothing they can really do to prevent it.

All they can do is try to remember what they see. Perhaps, in time, those fragments will help them understand what is happening within the greater mystery.
